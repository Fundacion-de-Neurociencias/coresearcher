# Counterexample search: pre-registered protocol (draft)

Status: written 2026-10-19, before any arm has run. Revision 1 (same day, still before any run) corrects the design:
the control is the exact evaluator as it is today, and the corpus is larger than the exhaustive threshold. The script
records this file's SHA-256 with every raw result, and scoring refuses results produced under a different protocol
hash.

## 1. Question

CoResearcher checks deductions exactly over a declared finite state space, but nothing in it searches systematically
for a state that refutes a claim the author did not expect to fail. Can a proposer (a language model) find such
states, and does a search that only counts confirmed states improve on a search with no proposer at all?

This protocol tests the search, not the formalization. Whether a natural-language claim has been formalized
faithfully is outside its scope; ADR-066 records that the automatic fidelity check is at chance (AUROC 0.524).

## 2. Rules fixed in advance

- A proposed state counts as a **counterexample** only when the exact evaluator (enumeration over the declared domain)
  confirms that it satisfies the assumptions and violates a claim. The proposer's own judgement never counts.
- A proposal that is not confirmed is reported as `UNCONFIRMED`, with the reason, and never as a finding.
- Every confirmed counterexample is a `verification` node in the evidence graph, with the evaluator named as the tool.

## 3. Corpus

Generated by a fixed script from seed `51000 + i`, for i = 0..199. Each item has 7 discrete variables `v0..v6`, each
with domain `{0, ..., 9}`, so Omega has 10⁷ states. This exceeds `max_states` (2 000 000, `formal_thresholds`), so the
exact evaluator refuses to build the hypothesis (`ThresholdExceeded`). That is the regime where a search is the only
available tool. Each item has one assumption, `lo <= v0 <= hi`, with `lo` in 0..4 and `hi` in lo+3..9.

- **Correct items (100).** The claims are implied by the assumption or by the domain: `lo <= v0 <= hi` and `v1 >= 0`.
  No state is forbidden.
- **Planted items (100).** The correct claims plus one claim `not (v0 == a and vj == b)`, with `a` in `[lo, hi]`,
  `j` in 1..6 and `b` in 0..9. The forbidden states are exactly `{v0 == a, vj == b}`: 10⁵ states, about 1 % of Omega.
  The pattern is recorded in a truth file that the scoring script reads and the proposer never sees.

Ground truth is therefore known by construction. The corpus is not a sample of real manuscripts.

## 4. Arms

- **E, exact evaluator (control, the status quo).** The hypothesis is built and evaluated exhaustively. Every item
  returns `BLOCKED` by construction, so detection is 0. This records what CoResearcher can do today on this regime.
- **R, random search.** 50 states drawn uniformly from the full domain, seed `53000 + i`. Each is confirmed or
  rejected by an exact point check. This is the null control for the proposer: a proposer must beat chance to count.
- **P, proposer.** A language model is given the variables, domains, assumption and claims, with no hint about which
  claim is wrong. It is asked for up to 10 candidate states per call, in 5 independent calls (seeds `55000 + 10·i + k`,
  temperature 0.7). Its candidates are checked by the same point check. The exact model tag is recorded.

**Point check.** A candidate is confirmed when it assigns every variable a value in its domain, satisfies the assumption,
and violates at least one claim, as evaluated by the `formal_hypothesis` predicate language. The check is exact for that
state. Malformed or out-of-domain candidates are rejected and counted, never repaired.

Budget per item: 50 candidate states for P and for R.

## 5. Metrics

- **Detection rate** on planted items: the share of items with at least one confirmed counterexample.
- **Confirmation rate** of proposals: confirmed states over all proposed states.
- **False detection rate** on correct items. Under the rule in section 2 this must be zero, and it is reported to check
  that the rule holds.
- **Unconfirmed rate** on correct items: the share of proposals that the evaluator rejects, as a measure of the noise
  the gate removes.

Uncertainty: 95% Wilson intervals for rates, and a paired bootstrap over items (10,000 resamples, seed `20261019`)
for the difference in detection rate between P and R, and between P and N.

## 6. Pre-registered criteria

- **Validity (V).** The false detection rate on correct items is exactly 0. If it is not, the point check is broken and
  no other criterion is read.
- **Null gate (G).** R's detection rate on planted items is reported with its interval. With 50 draws at about 1 %
  density, its expected value is about 40 %, which is the chance level the proposer must beat.
- **Proposer advantage (A).** The lower bound of the 95% bootstrap interval of `detection(P) − detection(R)` is above 0.
- **Search gain (S).** The lower bound of `detection(P) − detection(E)` is above 0. E is 0 by construction, so this
  asks whether P finds any planted error the exact evaluator cannot reach.

A criterion that is not met is reported as not met. Thresholds, budget, corpus generator and the proposer prompt
do not change after any result is seen.

## 7. Known limitations, stated in advance

- The 1 % density and the single planted pattern are design choices. A different density changes the chance level of R.
- Planted errors are synthetic. Real errors in published proofs are subtler and are not in the finite-domain format.
- A finite domain can only refute claims about the states it declares. Errors that need an unbounded domain, such as
  a quantifier over all integers, are outside this benchmark.
- Below the threshold, enumeration is exact and a search adds nothing. This protocol says nothing about that regime,
  where the answer is already the exhaustive verdict.
- A detection rate says nothing about how often real claims are wrong. The 5.9% and 1.5% figures in the social-media
  post are not used here; they come from a source we have not been able to read.
- The proposer may have seen similar logic in training. This is controlled only partly, by the random arm R.

## 8. Execution

Script: `scripts/benchmarks/run_counterexample_search.py`, with `run --arm E|R|P [--worlds a-b]` and `score`, resumable
like the v4 benchmark. Arms E and R need only the standard library and the formal predicate module. Arm P needs a local
model (`FORMALIZER_BASE_URL`, `FORMALIZER_MODEL`) and runs on the Legion (runbook, section 7).

## 9. Revision history

- Revision 0 (2026-10-19): first draft, committed. Its control was an exhaustive check over a small domain, where
  enumeration is already exact and a search adds nothing. Its budget and call counts were inconsistent.
- Revision 1 (2026-10-19, before any arm ran): the control is the exact evaluator in the regime it cannot decide
  (Omega above `max_states`); the corpus, the point check and the budget are as in sections 3 and 4.
