# Atlas Roadmap

Scientific Knowledge Infrastructure scaling plan.

---

## Scaling Targets

| Milestone | Claims | Hypotheses | Predictions | Trust |
|-----------|--------|-----------|------------|-----|
| Phase 1 | 100 | 10 | 1 | Initial validation |
| Phase 2 | 1,000 | 100 | 10 | Emerging patterns |
| Phase 3 | 10,000 | 1,000 | 100 | Established knowledge |
| Phase 4 | 100,000 | 10,000 | 1,000 | Strong consensus |

---

## What Grows with Scale

### At 1,000 Claims
- Trust scores stabilize (claims with consistent evidence get higher scores)
- Findings emerge as patterns across multiple claims
- Knowledge gaps become apparent

### At 10,000 Claims
- Predictions improve (more training data for models)
- Consensus levels reach "Moderate" for key findings
- Cross-domain insights appear

### At 100,000 Claims
- Competitive advantage emerges
- Unique hypotheses that humans wouldn't generate
- Predictive models for unknown questions

---

## Scientific Yield Score Evolution

Current: 0.56 (5 predictions / 9 claims)

Target progression:
- 100 papers: > 0.1 predictions per paper
- 1,000 papers: > 0.2 predictions per paper
- 10,000 papers: > 0.5 predictions per paper (emergent discovery)

---

## Success Criteria

The system produces **scientific value** when an external researcher considers that the generated `report.md` contains:

1. At least one hypothesis they wouldn't have thought of by reading abstracts
2. Predictions that are testable and non-trivial
3. Gaps that point to genuine research opportunities

---

## What Makes This Defensible

Unlike traditional literature databases, this infrastructure stores:
- **Questions** - What we're trying to understand
- **Hypotheses** - What we believe might be true
- **Predictions** - What we expect to observe
- **Evidence** - What we observed
- **Trust** - How confident we are
- **Provenance** - Where everything came from

This is the **scientific process itself**, not just its outputs.
