## 1. Why this input

The released reasoning summary for family 087 (Mahler, Part III) ends with a proof that is conditional on uniform
scalar inequalities. The manuscript "The Mahler Conjecture for General Convex Bodies" (openai/math, commit
`7ef5c0cc`) collects these inequalities in one place. They are Lemma *Profile identities* and Proposition *Scalar
inequalities*, `prop:scalar-input` in `build/sections/01-cones.tex`. The inequalities involve fixed one-variable
profiles built from the Gaussian distribution function, and fixed parameters that do not depend on the body or the
dimension. The parameters (b = .602, c = .365, r = .22, s = 3.6, …) are the same as in the summary.

The current formal and computational coverage of this input is as follows:

- **Lean.** Family 087's Lean targets cover only the symmetric case and its equality classification
  (`symmetric_mahler`, `symmetric_mahler_equality`). The general case is not formalized.
- **Author certificates.** Appendices 7–8, plus ten Python programs in `verification/` (interval enclosures and
  Bernstein bounds).
- **Our checks.** We did not run those programs. Under ADR-076 they are read as data and not executed. Our checks
  below are re-implemented from the statements.

## 2. Ledger

The scalar input has 20 obligations. Each inequality that we proved on a bounded window is split in two: the window
part, which we prove, and the rest of its domain, which we do not. A window proof never discharges the statement for
all x (ADR-076). Values come from `ledger.json` and `interval_proof.json`.

| Obligation | Status | Margin | Scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| Identities (N+2)g = d; d′ = 2xd − v′ − 2g′; Nd − v″ = −(N+2)(d + g″) | **DISCHARGED_SYMBOLIC** | exact | any p with p′ = φ |
| The 7 first-bullet inequalities, on \|x\| ≤ 8 | **DISCHARGED_INTERVAL** | certified lower bounds > 0 on 4,644 boxes | \|x\| ≤ 8 |
| The same 7, for \|x\| > 8 | **DISCHARGED_INTERVAL** | certified lower bounds > 0 on 16 boxes in t = 1/x | x ≥ 8 up to infinity (evenness for x < 0) |
| The 2 inequalities (3), on [−3, 3]² | **DISCHARGED_INTERVAL** | certified lower bounds > 0 on 167,121 box pairs | (z, x) ∈ [−3, 3]² |
| The same 2, outside [−3, 3]² | **DISCHARGED_INTERVAL** | certified lower bounds ≥ 0.040 and ≥ 0.976 on 7,344,616 box pairs | all of ℝ² outside [−3, 3]², every phase |
| (4), the segment inequality | CHECKED_NUMERIC + CERTIFIED_BY_AUTHOR | ratio of the two sides ≤ 0.73 (sampled) | segments in [−12, 12] |

The sampled margins (high precision, grid 0.02) of the first bullet are:

| Inequality | Smallest sampled margin |
|---|---|
| b − r² − d > 0 | ≈ 0.055 (at x = ±20) |
| \|v′ − x\| ≤ .319 | ≈ 0.001 |
| C ≤ −.341 | ≈ 3.4·10⁻⁴ (at x = 0) |
| S_q > 0 | ≈ 0.116 (at x = 0) |
| λt₋² + t₋C + K > 0 | ≈ 9·10⁻⁴ |
| λt₊² + t₊C + K < 0 | ≈ 7.5·10⁻⁴ |
| 2(−.37)C − .37² − 4λK > m*² | ≈ 0.009 |

**Claim status: CHECKED_NUMERIC, because of one obligation.** Everything in the proposition except the segment
inequality (4) is now proved by CoResearcher, independently of the authors' code:

1. the three profile identities (exactly);
2. all seven first-bullet inequalities for every real x: on |x| ≤ 8 by interval arithmetic, and on |x| ≥ 8 by
   Laurent–Taylor models in t = 1/x (§5);
3. both two-variable inequalities (3) on all of ℝ²: on [−3, 3]² by 2D branch and bound, and outside it by the phase
   reduction of §6.

The segment inequality (4) involves averages over segments. It rests on sampled evidence (the ratio of its two
sides is at most 0.73) plus the authors' appendix and programs.

The tight spots are the ones the sampling found, and the interval proof certifies them: C ≤ −.341 near x = 0, and
the quadratic conditions near x ≈ 0 and x ≈ 3.4. Negative controls show that tightening a constant (C ≤ −.342,
|v′ − x| ≤ .317) makes both the sampling and the interval proof fail.

## 3. Method notes

- **Precision.** The profile X = p/φ grows like e^{x²/2} and multiplies quantities of size e^{−x²}. Evaluation
  therefore uses 60 + 0.9x² decimal digits, and 1 − p is computed as erfc(x/√2)/2 rather than by subtraction. A
  first attempt at fixed precision produced values of order 10⁴⁰, which come from the arithmetic and not from the
  profiles.
- **Derivatives.** They are exact symbolic derivatives (SymPy), evaluated in mpmath. Segment averages use the
  trapezoid rule on the 0.02 grid.
- **Encoding check.** The identities first failed because p and 1 − p had been encoded as independent functions. They
  are proved once 1 − p is substituted back. That was an encoding error on our side, not a defect of the paper.

## 4. How the interval proof works

Details are in the docstring of `mahler_interval.py`.

1. **Stable forms on x ≥ 0.** With Y = (1 − p)/φ (the Mills ratio), u = 1 − p = φY and
   h₀(y) = (−log(1 − y) − y)/y² = Σ y^m/(m + 2), the profiles become

   - d = (φ + xp)² Y² h₀(u) + (1 − xY)² j,
   - g = (1 − p²Y²h₀(u) − Y²j)/2,
   - v = log p + log Y + x²/2 + log √(2π),

   with j = −1 + u + x²/2 + log √(2π) − log Y. They contain no exponentially large factor. Derivatives close over
   p′ = φ, φ′ = −xφ, Y′ = xY − 1, u′ = −φ and h_k′ = −φ h_{k+1}. SymPy proves four things:

   - these forms equal the paper's profiles;
   - d, g and v are even (x ↦ −x swaps p and 1 − p), so x ≥ 0 suffices;
   - the derivative rules;
   - the three identities.
2. **Rigorous inputs.** At a point, p and 1 − p come from the alternating erf series, with its remainder bound,
   in interval arithmetic. On a box, monotonicity gives the enclosures: p increases; φ, Y (= ∫₀^∞ e^{−xt−t²/2} dt)
   and u decrease; h_k has positive coefficients.
3. **Enclosures and branch and bound.** The first bullet uses the mean-value form f(m) + f′(box)·[−w/2, w/2]. The
   one-variable pieces of (3) use second-order Taylor forms, combined over box pairs in float intervals widened by
   one ulp per operation, with every constant enclosed exactly. A box is certified when every margin's lower bound
   is positive; otherwise it is bisected. Symmetry (z, x) ↦ (−z, −x) halves the two-variable domain.
4. **Trust base.** The proof relies on the correctness of mpmath's interval arithmetic (outward rounding) and of
   IEEE-754 round-to-nearest for the float stage. It does not use the authors' programs.

## 5. The tail x ≥ 8

The window method cannot reach infinity, and for large x it loses the asymptotic cancellations. On x ≥ 8 the proof
uses t = 1/x ∈ (0, 1/8]; t = 0 is x = ∞.

1. **Mills ratio with a rigorous remainder.** Y(x) = ∫₀^∞ e^{−xs} e^{−s²/2} ds. For y ≥ 0 the Taylor remainder of
   e^{−y} after n terms has the sign (−1)^n and modulus at most yⁿ/n!. Integrating term by term gives
   xY = Σ_{k<n} (−1)^k (2k−1)!! t^{2k} + θ (−1)^n (2n−1)!! t^{2n} with θ ∈ [0, 1]. We use n = 10.
2. **Exponentially small parts.** φx^K decreases for x² > K, so φ = w t^K with w ∈ [0, φ(8)·8^K], taking K = 40.
   We write h_k(u) = h_k(0) + u·Δ_k, with h_k(0) = k!/(k+2) exact and Δ_k enclosed by the mean-value theorem.
3. **Laurent–Taylor models in t.**
   - Exact rational coefficients for the parts that must cancel. log 2 and log π are kept as symbols until the end,
     so every negative power of t cancels exactly; the test suite checks this.
   - Interval coefficients for the uncertain parts.
   - A remainder R·t^m valid on all of [0, 1/8].
4. **log Y = log t + log Ψ.** log t enters linearly, always with a positive power of t, and t^e·log t is monotone
   on (0, 1/8].

All seven margins are certified on 16 boxes covering [0, 1/8], in under a second. Two independent cross-checks
confirm the models: at x = 8 and x = 20 they enclose the independently computed profiles, and the Mills bracket
contains the true ratio at x = 8, 12 and 40. A negative control (C ≤ −1/2, which fails as x → ∞ because C → −1/2
from above) is not certified.

## 6. The two-variable inequalities outside [−3, 3]²

1. **Phase reduction.** Write π = r·u(θ) with θ = w·asinh(x/s), u = (cos, sin) and u⊥ = (−sin, cos). Then
   Sπ = r·(a·u + b·u⊥), with a = 2 + θ′² and b = xθ′ − θ″. The inequalities see π only through two dot products:

   - Sπ(z)·π(x) = r²(a_z cos Δ + b_z sin Δ);
   - Sπ(z)·Sπ(x) = r²((a_z a_x + b_z b_x) cos Δ + (b_z a_x − a_z b_x) sin Δ),

   where Δ = θ(x) − θ(z). SymPy proves these identities and |Sπ|² = r²(a² + b²). Leaving Δ free, the dot products
   range over ρ(z)·r·[−1, 1] and ρ(z)ρ(x)·[−1, 1], with ρ = |Sπ| (Cauchy–Schwarz, attained). Dropping the phase only
   adds cases. Every remaining quantity (q, S_q, v″, ρ) is even, so the region becomes z, x ≥ 0 with
   max(z, x) > 3.
2. **One-dimensional enclosures on [0, ∞).**
   - On [0, 6], second-order Taylor forms on boxes bisected until S_q is narrower than 0.01 and q, v″, ρ narrower
     than 0.005. This gives 2,779 boxes in total.
   - On [6, ∞), the Laurent–Taylor models of §5 with X₀ = 6, 16 Mills terms and φ = w·t³², on 64 boxes in t, the
     last one reaching x = ∞.
   - ρ is evaluated in t = 1/x for x ≥ 1, where it has no repeated variable.
3. **Pair check.** Every pair of boxes outside the window is checked, with the free cosine split into 16 intervals
   for the first inequality. The check uses float intervals widened by one ulp per operation, with exact constants.

Two failed runs, both on our side, are recorded in the history. First, boxes on [0, 3] were too wide, so S_q came
out up to 2.1 wide. Second, ρ was evaluated with a repeated x on large bounded boxes. The final run certifies all
7,344,616 pairs.

## 7. The segment inequality (4): what is checked so far

The authors prove (4) in Appendix 8 (`prop:segment-estimate`). They reduce it to scalar budget inequalities, then
split the plane of endpoints into four regimes: short widths, tails, middle widths (with Bernstein certificates) and
larger widths. We follow their decomposition and recheck each step independently.

**Proved here (DISCHARGED_SYMBOLIC, `mahler_segment.py`):**

- The closed-form mean of the circle part over a segment in t = asinh(x/s): e^{iwm}(A_h + iB_h tanh m).
- A_h² + B_h² = (1 + sin²(wh)/sinh²h)/(1 + w²). Hence f₀* = r²(1 − A_h² − B_h²) > 0, and f₀* bounds the circle
  variance from below.
- The endpoint distances: avg|π − π_±|² = p_a ± p_d.
- The equivalence of (4) with the budget (s0), including the variance decomposition.
- The chord-kernel (trapezoid) identity, and the two identities behind the bounds on its weight W.
- The short-width budget (h ≤ .16), evaluated in exact rationals: 73838672078372996926982859 /
  133016573919372558593750000 ≈ 0.5551088. This exceeds the authors' .55510 and the cost .54. A negative control
  (raising the bound on |Ċ| to .5) makes the budget fall below the cost.

**Also proved here:**

- **The profile bounds the short-width budget uses** (`lem:scalar-ranges`, `mahler_ranges.py`): |q̇| ≤ .193,
  |Ċ| ≤ .247, |n_C| ≤ .79, |n_q| ≤ .53 and U_* ≤ .54 r²w², where dots are t-derivatives and n_F = (s² + x²)F″.
  They hold for every real x. On |x| ≤ 8 they are certified by interval branch and bound (48,550 boxes). The
  tightest certified margin is about 2·10⁻⁶, for |Ċ| near x ≈ 1.7. On |x| ≥ 8 they are certified by the
  Laurent–Taylor models in t = 1/x (16 boxes), with the factors x and x² multiplied inside the models.
- **The six elementary inequalities of the short-width cost table** (`short_width_elementary`):
  - the weight constants 1.507 and 1.012;
  - sinh²h ≥ h² + h⁴/3;
  - sin²y ≤ y² − y⁴/3 + 2y⁶/45 for |y| ≤ 2;
  - 0 ≤ sin y − y cos y ≤ y³/3;
  - 0 ≤ coth h − 1/h ≤ h/3;
  - avg β² ≤ 1/3 + .05h².

  They are proved from series sign arguments, coefficient comparisons and interval arithmetic.

So every numeric and algebraic input of the short-width regime (h ≤ .16) is now independently proved. The
short deductions that combine them (Cauchy–Schwarz, the chord identity, the cost table) follow the manuscript, and
we have not machine-checked them as a single statement.

**The middle and large widths, by a direct check (`mahler_segment_box.py`).** On the bounded region
{|l| ≤ 1.4, y ≤ 3.6, h ≥ .16, m ≥ 0} (l = m − h, y = m + h in t = asinh(x/s)) we do not reproduce the authors'
Bernstein certificates. Instead we check the exact form of (4) directly:

    e_K + Γ + (X² + Y²)/(4λ) + t₊(p_a + b_a) < (1 + t₊)V_u,   V_u = r²(1 − A_h² − B_h² tanh²m) + V_q.

- The t-axis is cut into cells of width 1/1280. On each cell, K, C, q and q² are enclosed by second-order Taylor
  forms in x (|x| ≤ 8) or by the Laurent–Taylor models (|x| ≥ 8). The profiles are even, so negative cells mirror
  positive ones.
- Segment averages use the density cosh t, so they are uniform averages in x. Each one is a sum of cell integrals
  plus two partial end cells, and each partial cell is enclosed by [0, width] times the cell enclosure. Prefix sums
  carry an explicit bound on the float rounding.
- Every pair of endpoint cells in the region is checked in float intervals, widened by one ulp per operation. The
  circle factors A_h, B_h, cos wh, sin wh and tanh m are enclosed with mpmath. Pairs that fail are split into
  8 × 8 sub-pairs at cell width 1/10240 and checked again.
- Reflection t ↦ −t preserves both sides of (4), so m ≥ 0 suffices.

Result (`examples/mahler-obligations/segment_box_proof.json`, about 12 minutes with 8 workers):

- all 12,534,436 cell pairs are certified;
- 17,596 of them needed the refinement into 1,070,056 sub-pairs. They lie next to the short-width boundary
  (h ≈ .16, l ≈ .23–.59), where both sides of (4) are about .008 and the absolute margin is about .002;
- the smallest certified lower bound of right − left is 3.5·10⁻⁸;
- the true worst ratio left/right in the region, from midpoint values, is about .76.

DISCHARGED_INTERVAL.

8 workers. See [PROOF_PIPELINE.md](../PROOF_PIPELINE.md).
