# Hypothesis inspiration: pre-registered protocol

Status: drafted on 2026-10-20. Configuration revision of the same day, before any measurement: the available model
environment is Ollama with Qwen 2.5 only, so the frontier comparators and the frontier judge are removed (sections 4
and 5.4). Nothing in this file has been run. The script that implements it will record this file's SHA-256 with every raw result, and scoring refuses results produced
under a different protocol hash.

Reference point: DiscoveryRSI (arXiv:2610.23839, Zhong et al., PhAI Labs, 2026) reports 6.3 % literature inspiration
for DiscoveryRSI-27B against 2.5 % for GPT and 2.3 % for Claude Opus 5. The authors state that the measure is
descriptive, that it uses no significance tests, and that it does not show researcher exposure or adoption. This
protocol measures the same construct under conditions that can support a comparison.

## 1. Question

Do hypotheses generated by CoResearcher's own pipeline correspond to later published work more often than hypotheses
from a comparator, when both are scored by the same rules, with a judge that has been checked against humans and with
retrieval that does not depend on how the proposal is formatted?

## 2. What is not claimed

- Correspondence is not adoption, influence or priority. A later paper may match a hypothesis without having seen it.
- A high rate does not establish that the hypothesis is true, valuable or novel.
- The measure depends on the reference corpus, the date rule and the retrieval gate. Each is fixed below, and every
  result is reported with them.

## 3. Definition of a match

A generated hypothesis version H matches a reference R if all three hold:

1. **Temporal.** R was published after the recorded training cutoff of the generating model, using the same date rule
   for every arm (section 5.3).
2. **Retrieved.** R is among the top-k candidates for H, where k = 20, retrieved with the query of section 5.2.
3. **Judged.** The judge (section 5.4) answers yes to: "Does R supply a concrete experimental design, comparator,
   measurement, or testable evidence gap that bears on H?" A yes requires a passage in R that says so. A paraphrase of
   the topic is not enough.

H is a match if at least one R satisfies all three. The rate is matches over all generated versions in the arm.
Versions that fail to parse are counted in the denominator and are never dropped.

## 4. Arms and matched conditions

Every arm answers the same research questions, drawn once from the question bank (section 5.1), under the same
budget, the same evidence pack and the same output schema.

- **C1, CoResearcher base model** (no self-improvement).
- **C2, CoResearcher after one round** of the pipeline's own improvement procedure. **Not implemented:** CoResearcher
  has no per-hypothesis credit assignment, no training on research trajectories and no harness replay.
- **C3, CoResearcher after two rounds.** Not implemented, for the same reason.
- **C4, same compute, no credit assignment and no harness replay.** Not implemented.

Consequence: the primary contrast (C3 against F1) and the mechanism contrast (C3 against C4) cannot be run. The only
runnable arm is C1, so the protocol supports only an exploratory rate for C1 against its random-reference floor
(section 5.6). No criterion is read from it, and it says nothing about DiscoveryRSI.
- **F1, frontier comparator A (removed in this configuration)**, named by exact version and serving date.
- **F2, frontier comparator B (removed in this configuration)**, named by exact version and serving date.

The model names, versions, dates and prompts of F1 and F2 are recorded in the raw records. A comparator is included
only if it can be run under the same budget. If not, it is reported as not run, never estimated.

## 5. Measurement procedure

### 5.1 Research questions

200 research questions drawn by a fixed seed (`61000`) from a bank frozen before any arm runs. Questions are stratified
across four life-science domains, 50 each. No question is written after seeing outputs.

### 5.2 Retrieval query

For each hypothesis, a fixed extraction prompt, identical for all arms, produces a query of at most 300 characters from
the hypothesis claim and its declared variables. The query is built from the whole proposal, not its first N
characters. This removes the format sensitivity the DiscoveryRSI authors measured for Claude Opus 5, where retrieval
read only the first 6 000 characters of a 41 134-character proposal.

### 5.3 Reference corpus and date rule

A single reference corpus is built once, from a citation traversal with a fixed number of hops, from a fixed seed set
of anchor papers per domain. The corpus is identical for all arms. The date rule is the release date of each model
version, recorded exactly. Where only a month is known for a serving identifier, the month is recorded and the
boundary is stated; no boundary is guessed.

### 5.4 Judge

- The judge is **`qwen2.5-coder:32b`** served by Ollama, exact tag and digest recorded in every raw record. It is not
  the generator of C1 (`qwen2.5:14b-instruct-q4_K_M`). It belongs to the same model family as the generator, so its
  agreement with the generator is correlated, and this is reported with every result.
- The judge sees the full text of R and the full text of H. It reads no other arm's output and knows nothing about
  which arm H belongs to. H is presented under a random identifier.
- The judge returns yes or no and a quoted passage from R. A yes without a passage is recorded as no.

### 5.5 Human validation of the judge

A stratified random sample of 300 judged pairs is drawn with seed `61001`: 100 judged yes, 100 judged no and 100 from
the random-reference control (section 5.6). Two domain experts, blind to the judge's answer and to the arm, label each
pair. A third expert adjudicates disagreements.

- Inter-rater agreement between the two experts is reported as Cohen's kappa.
- The judge's sensitivity and specificity against the adjudicated labels are reported with Wilson intervals.
- **Gate:** the judge is used for the primary result only if its specificity is at least 0.90 and its agreement with
  the adjudicated labels has a Cohen's kappa of at least 0.60. Otherwise the primary result is the human-adjudicated
  rate on the full set of matched candidates, and the judge's rate is reported as secondary.

### 5.6 Random-reference control

For each hypothesis, one post-cutoff reference is drawn at random from the same domain and judged with the same
procedure. The judge's yes rate on these pairs is the false-positive rate of the procedure. It is reported for every
arm next to its rate, and no rate is corrected by it, so that a reader can see what the judge says about unrelated papers.

## 6. Sample size

For the primary contrast, C3 against F1 at rates near 6.3 % and 2.5 %, with two-sided α = 0.05 and power 0.80, the
normal approximation gives **457 hypothesis versions per arm**. The calculation is in section 6.1. Arms with fewer
versions are reported as exploratory, with their intervals, and no criterion is read from them.

Because versions from one research branch share ancestry, the effective sample is smaller than the count. Uncertainty
is therefore estimated by a **cluster bootstrap over research branches** (10 000 resamples, seed `61002`), not by
treating versions as independent.

### 6.1 Calculation

With p1 = 0.063, p2 = 0.025, z(α/2) = 1.96 and z(β) = 0.8416:
n = (z(α/2)·√(2p̄(1−p̄)) + z(β)·√(p1(1−p1) + p2(1−p2)))² / (p1 − p2)², with p̄ = (p1 + p2)/2 = 0.044.
This gives n ≈ 456.4, rounded up to 457 per arm. The same formula is used to report the minimum detectable difference
for every arm that is not powered for the primary contrast.

## 7. Criteria fixed in advance

- **Validity (V).** The judge gate in section 5.5 is met. If it is not, only the human-adjudicated rate is read.
- **Format invariance (I).** For a random 100 hypotheses per arm, the rate computed with the full-proposal query and
  the rate computed with a title-only query differ by less than 2 percentage points. If not, the result is reported
  as format-dependent and not as a comparison of models.
- **Primary (P).** Lower bound of the 95 % cluster-bootstrap interval of rate(C3) − rate(F1) is above 0.
- **Mechanism (M).** Lower bound of rate(C3) − rate(C4) is above 0. This is the test of whether the self-improvement
  mechanisms add anything beyond the same compute spent without them.
- **Baseline floor (B).** The random-reference rate for every arm is reported. A primary result whose rate is not
  clearly above that floor is reported as not distinguishable from relatedness within the domain.

A criterion not met is reported as not met. Thresholds, the judge, the query, the corpus and the sample sizes do not
change after any result is seen.

## 8. Reporting

For every arm: n versions, n branches, rate with cluster-bootstrap 95 % interval, random-reference rate, judge
sensitivity and specificity, kappa, the per-domain rates, the number of unparsed outputs, and the exact model names,
dates and prompt hashes. A result is never reported without its denominator.

## 9. Known limitations, stated in advance

- A judge can be wrong in both directions. The human validation bounds the error only on the sampled pairs.
- The reference corpus depends on the citation traversal. A paper not reachable from the seed set cannot match.
- Frontier comparators are served through interfaces whose internal settings are not all visible. The protocol records
  what is visible.
- Literature correspondence is retrospective. The prospective test, which freezes hypotheses before researchers review
  them and then follows the investigations, is outside this protocol.
